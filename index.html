Partial control: safe sets and safety functions

In transient chaos, orbits of a map eventually escape from a region Q. When the system is affected by bounded noise, escapes happen even faster. The dynamics follow

x(n+1) = f(x(n)) + ξ(n) + u(n)

where |ξn| ≤ ξ₀ is a bounded disturbance and |un| ≤ u₀ is a bounded control. Even when u₀ < ξ₀, that is, when the control is weaker than the disturbance, escapes can be avoided by keeping the orbit inside a special subset of Q called the safe set, drawn in blue in every plot. From any point of the safe set, and whatever the disturbance, a control within the bound takes the orbit back to the safe set.

Safety function

The safety function U(x) gives, for each point of Q, the minimum control bound that keeps its orbit in Q forever. The safe set for a control bound u₀ is the set of points where U(x) ≤ u₀, so a safe set exists only if u₀ ≥ min U.

1D maps

Tent, logistic, cubic, Gauss and other maps, or your own equation. Follow controlled and uncontrolled orbits on the cobweb diagram, step through single iterations to see f(x), ξ and u as arrows, or switch to the time series. The safety function view plots U(x): moving u₀ floods its valleys, and the flooded intervals are the safe set. The region Q can be made of up to three intervals, and the computation can be exported as Python and MATLAB scripts.

2D maps

Hénon, Lozi, Ikeda and Zaslavsky maps, and the Lorenz system controlled each time its trajectory crosses the Poincaré section z = 19, which makes the problem two-dimensional. The safe set is computed in your browser on a grid. Drag on the plot to zoom into its structure, edit the limits of Q on the axes, and overlay controlled orbits, uncontrolled orbits and the chaotic saddle. The safety function view draws U(x, y) as a surface you can rotate, with a plane at u₀ that leaves the safe set below it.

3D systems

Safe sets of the Lorenz system, the McCann-Yodzis ecological model and a delayed Hénon map, precomputed for several control intervals and bounds and shown as surfaces you can rotate. The red surface is the asymptotic safe set, where controlled trajectories end up. Cut opens them along any of the three axes to show their sections. For these systems only safe sets are shown, not safety functions.
